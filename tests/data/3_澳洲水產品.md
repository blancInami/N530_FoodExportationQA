## Objective

This questionnaire is designed for the Taiwan’s competent authority to provide information to the department about the regulation and enforcement of bivalve mollusc production and processing in Taiwan. This information will be used to:

* assess whether the regulatory system in Taiwan for bivalve mollusc production and processing provides an equivalent food safety outcome to the system in Australia
* assess whether an equivalence agreement can be developed with certification to provide assurance that bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products from Taiwan meet the applicable standards in Australia and do not pose a risk to human health
* prepare a draft equivalence assessment report that will be provided to Taiwan for comment before completing the equivalence assessment.

This questionnaire seeks information about Taiwan’s bivalve mollusc production and processing systems as follows:

* Part A – Inspection and Certification System Infrastructure
* Part B – Control systems for bivalve mollusc production, transport and processing
* Part C – Primary production requirements for bivalve molluscs
* Part D – Microbiological limits that apply to bivalve molluscs
* Part E – Maximum limits that apply to bivalve molluscs
* Part F – Laboratory analysis and competence
* Part G – Investigation of illness associated with bivalve molluscs
* Part H – Labelling requirements

## Scope

The equivalence assessment only applies to bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products produced and processed in Taiwan. If you intend to export bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products to Australia that are grown, harvested or processed in another country, contact the department before completing this questionnaire.

### Bivalve molluscs

Bivalve molluscs are clams, cockles, mussels, oysters, pipi and scallops.

### Bivalve mollusc products

Bivalve mollusc products are foods containing any bivalve molluscs. For example, frozen meals that contain bivalve molluscs as an ingredient.

### Exclusions

Bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products that are either:

* dried (not semi-dried), or
* both retorted and shelf stable.

## Eligibility to export bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products to Australia

Jurisdictions wanting to export bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products to Australia can apply for assessment of whether their bivalve mollusc production and processing system provides an equivalent food safety outcome to the system in Australia. If the jurisdiction can meet Australia’s [biosecurity requirements](https://www.awe.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/online-services/bicon) and demonstrate equivalence for managing food safety risks, a government certification arrangement will be established to facilitate trade.

As part of negotiating certification arrangements, recognition will be given to establishments that are permitted to export to countries that have equivalent shellfish sanitation programs and competent authority oversight as Australia. This includes the United States and member states of the European Union.

A jurisdiction must be able to demonstrate that:

1. controls are in place to classify and monitor growing areas for bivalve molluscs
2. controls are in place for the safe production and processing of bivalve molluscs, including standards and control measures for
3. the harvesting, relaying, depuration and storage of bivalve molluscs that effectively control pathogenic microorganisms and biotoxins
4. the temperature control of bivalve molluscs
5. the processing of bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products that effectively control pathogenic microorganisms.
6. government controls and export conditions are in place to ensure
7. the bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products have been produced under a food safety management system that effectively controls the hazards
8. the bivalve molluscs are only harvested from areas that have been classified as suitable for producing bivalve molluscs for human consumption and were open at the time of harvesting
9. the bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products are monitored to verify their safety and suitability.
10. it has a government authority (or authorities) that
11. verifies and enforces compliance with national standards, government controls and export conditions for bivalve molluscs and bivalve mollusc products
12. is able to supply valid government certificates attesting to compliance with the above.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| area | an area where bivalve molluscs are grown or harvested. |
| batch | a quantity of bivalve molluscs harvested from a particular harvesting area (e.g., marine farm, lease or designated wild shellstock harvest area) and with the same harvest date. |
| bivalve molluscs | clams, cockles, mussels, oysters, pipi and scallops. |
| bivalve mollusc handler | a person who engages in or supervises the primary production of bivalve molluscs, for a business. |
| bivalve mollusc products | foods containing any bivalve molluscs (for example, frozen meals that contain bivalve molluscs as an ingredient), but exclude those that are:   * dried (not semi-dried); and * both retorted and shelf stable. |
| business | a business, enterprise or activity that involves the primary production of bivalve molluscs intended for sale. |
| competent authority | the government authority or official body authorised by the government that is responsible for the setting of regulatory food safety requirements and/or for the organisation of official controls including enforcement. |
| depuration | a process using a controlled environment to reduce the level of certain pathogenic  organisms that may be present in live shellfish. |
| ready-to-eat bivalve molluscs or bivalve mollusc products in which growth of *Listeria monocytogenes* will not occur | 1. the bivalve molluscs or bivalve molluscs products have a pH less than 4.4 regardless of water activity; or 2. the bivalve molluscs or bivalve molluscs products have a water activity less than 0.92 regardless of pH; or 3. the bivalve molluscs or bivalve molluscs products have a pH less than 5.0 in combination with a water activity of less than 0.94; or 4. the bivalve molluscs or bivalve molluscs products have a refrigerated shelf life no greater than 5 days (includes frozen bivalve molluscs that have a refrigerated shelf life of no greater than 5 days once thawed); or 5. the bivalve molluscs or bivalve molluscs products are frozen (stored and sold frozen to be thawed by the consumer immediately before consumption); or 6. it has been be validated that the level of *Listeria monocytogenes* will not increase by greater than 0.5 log cfu/g over the stated shelf life of the bivalve molluscs or bivalve mollusc products. |
| food safety control system | the exporting country’s laws and systems for regulating and enforcing bivalve mollusc and bivalve mollusc product production and processing. |
| foodborne disease | a disease that is likely to be transmitted through consumption of contaminated food. |
| growing area | a marine or enclosed body of water (i.e. bay, harbour, gulf, cove, lagoon, inlet, estuary or river) in which commercial species of bivalve molluscs grow naturally or are grown by means of aquaculture. A growing area may consist of one or more harvest areas. |
| harvest area | an area that has been designated by a competent authority for the purpose of growing and/or harvesting commercial quantities of shellstock and may include wildstock or aquacultured shellstock. |
| harvesting | the capture or taking of bivalve molluscs and includes the capture or taking of bivalve molluscs from an enclosure or pond used in aquaculture. |
| inputs | any feed, chemicals or other substances used in, or in connection with, the primary production of bivalve molluscs. |
| premises | any premises including land, vehicles, parts of structures, tents, stalls and other temporary structures, vessels, pontoons, and any other place declared by the competent authority to be a premises, kept or used for the primary production of bivalve molluscs (exclusively or otherwise), regardless of whether the premises are owned by the proprietor, including premises used principally as a private dwelling. |
| primary production of seafood, including bivalve molluscs | 1. growing, cultivation, picking, harvesting, collection or catching of seafood; or 2. growing on of seafood; or 3. transportation or delivery of seafood; or 4. holding of live seafood;   and includes processing of seafood. |
| processing of seafood, including bivalve molluscs | 1. the killing, dismembering, filleting or cutting into portions, gill or gutting, or skinning of seafood; and 2. the depuration of shellfish and crustaceans; and 3. the shucking or peeling of seafood; and 4. the cooking, including steaming or boiling, of crustaceans; and 5. the brining of seafood; and 6. the packing, treating, washing, freezing, refrigeration or storing of seafood; and 7. other similar activities. |
| relaying | the transfer of bivalve molluscs from one area to another for the reduction of contaminants in the bivalve molluscs. |
| sanitary survey | the (written) evaluation of all actual and potential pollution sources and environmental factors which may affect shellfish harvest area water quality and hence the shellfish. |
| seafood | all aquatic vertebrates and aquatic invertebrates intended for human consumption, but excludes amphibians, mammals, reptiles, and aquatic plants. |
| shellstock | live shellfish in the shell. |
| shoreline survey | a survey conducted by a competent authority to identify and record pollution sources within the catchment of a shellfish growing area which contaminate or have the potential to contaminate the water quality of a shellfish harvest area. |
| temperature control | maintaining bivalve molluscs at a temperature of:   1. 5°C, or below if this is necessary to minimise the growth of infectious or toxigenic microorganisms in the food so that the microbiological safety of the food will not be adversely affected for the time the food is at that temperature; or 2. another temperature – if the business demonstrates that maintenance of the food at this temperature for the period of time for which it will be so maintained, will not adversely affect the microbiological safety of the food. |
